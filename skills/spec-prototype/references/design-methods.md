# Design methods — route by uncertainty

Use after product grounding when a consequential Product Experience Model choice
is open or a validated design is weak. Methods help the designer think and make
evidence; they do not own workflow, approval or product truth.

## Method discipline

For the active decision:

1. **Discover** actual product, user, context, content and reference evidence.
2. **Define** the owning question, intended effect, constraints and falsification
   condition.
3. **Develop** inspectable answers on the same representative task/content when a
   real alternative exists.
4. **Deliver** by testing, rejecting or refining and returning the result to the
   existing owner.

This Double Diamond shape is iterative and optional at local scale. A settled or
mechanically fixed value needs no fake divergence. If the specimen reveals the
wrong problem, return to Define or Discover; finishing a phase never forces the
design forward.

Distinguish expression freedom—how the product communicates and feels—from
interaction freedom—what users must learn to operate. Preserve familiar behavior
where recognition and speed matter. Spend novelty where a product relationship,
task or emotion benefits enough to justify learning and implementation cost.

## Eight-lens router

Scan all eight lenses for consequential gaps, then load only the methods that own
the current risk.

| Lens | Questions that trigger deeper work | Method / retained result |
|---|---|---|
| Value and outcomes | Purpose, audience, value or success is ambiguous | [Product understanding](product-understanding.md) → Product Thesis, jobs/outcomes and open evidence |
| Research and context | A current fact, mental model, environment or user claim could change the choice | [Research](research.md) or [experience validation](experience-validation.md) → decision-linked evidence and limits |
| Objects and content | Terms, entities, relationships, authority, lifecycle or content structure are unclear | [IA and interaction](ia-interaction.md) → Object/Content Model |
| Journeys and service | End-to-end task, role/channel handoff, interruption or backstage dependency is unclear | [IA and interaction](ia-interaction.md) → Journey/State/Service Model |
| IA and Surface Topology | Organization, navigation, page necessity, simultaneous context or responsive transformation is uncertain | [IA and interaction](ia-interaction.md) → derived Surface Map and walkthrough |
| Interaction, usability, accessibility and trust | Operation model, form, data task, latency, control, risk, inclusive use or recovery is consequential | craft routes below → Contract behavior and evidence |
| Integrated expression | Content voice, brand, hierarchy, type, color, imagery, components or motion does not yet form one product language | [Visual craft](design-methods/visual-craft.md) and [design language](design-language.md) → Design Proposition/Foundation |
| Prototype, evaluation, system and handoff | Fidelity, testing, reusable system relationships or implementation boundary is uncertain | [Quality bar](quality-bar.md), [artifact lifecycle](artifact-lifecycle.md), [handoff](handoff.md) → evidence, retained contracts and packet |

Record a lens as applicable, inherited, unknown or out of scope only when that
status helps coverage or handoff. Do not create an eight-section report for every
repair.

## Craft routes

### Visual, brand, content and motion

Read [visual craft](design-methods/visual-craft.md) when developing a Design
Proposition, typography/space/color system, composition, image world, iconography
or cross-surface expression. Use representative content and tasks. A reference
contributes a mechanism and conditions, not a brand-name style instruction.

### Interaction and power use

Read [interaction craft](design-methods/interaction-power.md) for direct,
structured, generative or keyboard-first operation models; perceived performance;
interruption; focus; sensory feedback; or component behavior. Return concrete
states, transitions and acceptance to the Slice Contract.

### Forms and product voice

Read [form ergonomics](design-methods/form-ergonomics.md) for entry, validation,
dirty-state protection, correction and recovery, or when copy must help users act
under different emotional conditions.

### Data and repeated work

Read [data and task design](design-methods/data-information.md) for scanning,
comparison, filtering, search, sorting, batch action, tables and device-specific
representation. Preserve the same object semantics across responsive forms.

### Resilience, trust and inclusive adaptation

Read [resilience and trust](design-methods/resilience-trust.md) for consequential
actions, AI agency, permissions, collaboration, notification hierarchy, untrusted
input, offline/degraded behavior, text scaling, input changes, locale or assistive
semantics. Derive applicable risk states rather than applying a universal matrix.

## Generative choices

Choose a locus of invention from the product question:

- **Convention-led** when speed, familiarity, platform behavior or inherited
  identity dominates. Adapt a proven mechanism to the actual content and task.
- **Innovation-led** when a neglected relationship, emotional experience or
  operation problem warrants a new mechanism. Explain the benefit and learning
  cost, then test it against a competent conventional baseline.
- **Mixed** when continuity matters in one layer and invention can earn its cost
  in another. Name both boundaries.

Physical, cultural, editorial, spatial, material, narrative and interaction
references are open lenses, not a catalogue. Translate inspiration into an
observable relationship. Do not replace one fixed style menu with another or use
random novelty as creativity.

When expression direction is new or reopened, route the **local Design
Proposition loop** in [design language](design-language.md): synthesize
Qualitative Design DNA from the product basis, use Real-World Mapping only when
it reduces uncertainty, establish the Signature Relationship, express it as
Signature Craft on one representative specimen, then test transfer and
falsification. This is not a second product-design mainline. Double Diamond,
OOUX, service design, usability methods and craft lenses may enter wherever they
own the uncertainty; every result returns to the existing Product, Surface Map,
Foundation, Contract or Review owner.

## Divergence Gate (Challenge before converging)

Do not allow "settled by evidence" to become an excuse for unexamined conventional
clichés. On high-consequence surface or interaction decisions where only one obvious
answer has surfaced, the designer must pass through a deliberate **Divergence Gate**:

1. **Axis Inversion**: Reorganize the primary information/interaction axis (e.g. from
   a sequential step flow to a spatial workspace, or from a passive status monitor
   to a direct-manipulation steering wheel).
2. **Constraint Inversion**: Invert a key assumption (e.g. assume extreme screen
   density, zero-latency feedback, or keyboard-only emergency operation) and test if
   the core relationship survives.
3. **Antithetical Metaphor**: Counter the default metaphor with a contrasting one
   (e.g. if the default is an "administrative dashboard", test an "artisan's workbench"
   or "field logbook").
4. **Signature Craft Focus**: Select the single interaction moment that defines the
   character of the product, and articulate how its typography, spatial hierarchy,
   and motion create genuine brand and emotional resonance.

Document the challenged alternative and the exact reason it was rejected or integrated.
True creative tension comes from rigorous divergence tested against uncompromising
utility.

Every consequential proposition connects product thesis/question, generative
mechanism, representative specimen, deliberately retained convention, Signature
Relationship, expected benefit, cost/learning burden and falsification/transfer
test. Candidate quantity follows uncertainty.

## Method lineage and routing

Use Double Diamond for divergence/convergence, the Elements of UX for checking
strategy-to-expression dependencies, OOUX/ORCA for object-centered structure,
direct manipulation for operation feedback, Nielsen/Norman heuristics for
usability, information design for hierarchy and density, and service design for
cross-role/channel continuity when useful. Impeccable is an optional craft review
lens. DTCG is token interchange at the system/handoff layer. None is a mandatory
sequence or visual answer.

The Elements of UX is a dependency lens, not a second workflow: strategy and
scope must support the object/content and journey models; those models must
support the surface skeleton and interaction; the resulting surface and visual
language must make the intended task legible. When a lower layer exposes a
missing prerequisite, return the finding to the existing owning Product, Journey,
Surface, Foundation, Contract or Review record rather than inventing an
intermediate artifact.

Apply Nielsen/Norman heuristics as observable acceptance, not named authority.
For the representative task, state the task goal and the first reachable action;
for each applicable task state, require visible system status and feedback;
for failure, require understandable error information and prevention where
feasible; and for recovery, require a safe undo, cancel, retry or re-entry path
with preserved context where the job needs it. A direct-manipulation proposal
also needs a familiar non-gesture alternative when input, accessibility or
recoverability requires one. Record the acceptance and any unresolved risk in
the existing Surface, Contract or Review owner.

## Completion criterion

A method has earned its place when it changes an owning decision, produces
inspectable evidence or makes an explicit risk/unknown sharper. Return that result
to Product, Surface Map, Foundation, Contract, Review or Handoff. Mentioning a
method or filling a template is not completion.
