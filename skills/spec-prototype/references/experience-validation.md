# Investigate and test an experience decision

Read when an unresolved user need, findability or comprehension claim determines a
choice, or when planning/evaluating user research. External source retrieval stays
with research.md; this method owns study design and interpretation. Apply the
shared Double Diamond in design-methods.md and retain results in the existing
product/research/Review records. Select the lightest method that can change the
highest-risk current decision; the Product Experience Model does not require
research theater at every node.

| Phase | Work and hand-back |
|---|---|
| Discover | Identify the actual audience, task episode, context and existing evidence. Separate reported preferences, observed behavior and causal explanations. List competing explanations for the consequential difficulty. |
| Define | Name the decision this study could change, target participants/context, task outcome and observable failure. Choose the method below; state what its evidence cannot establish. |
| Develop | Pilot a neutral task with realistic data; compare feasible design hypotheses on equivalent tasks. Revise an ambiguous study instruction before attributing failure to a design. |
| Deliver | Retain observations, assistance given, outcome and interpretation separately. Map each finding to the responsible design choice, confidence and a follow-up observation; recheck the affected task after revision. |

## Choose evidence by the question

- Unknown work or motivation: ask about a recent concrete episode or observe work;
  inspect artifacts and exceptions before suggesting a solution. An interview can
  establish a report, not the measured frequency or causal effect of a behavior.
- Category/label fit: use card sorting to explore organization, tree testing for
  an organization hypothesis, or first-click tasks for visible findability. A
  successful click alone cannot establish full task completion.
- Comprehension, operation and recovery: use a task-based usability session with
  people matching the relevant audience. State the goal/context without naming
  the control or navigation route. Observe first unaided action, hesitation,
  wrong turns, corrections and outcome before asking why.
- Comparative efficiency: define success, assistance, errors and time boundaries
  before measuring. Control task/data difficulty and learning/order effects.
  Small convenience samples support diagnosis, not population-wide improvement.
- No participants available: perform a labeled expert walkthrough and produce a
  ready-to-run study protocol. Use scenarios to challenge a design, not synthetic
  personas as evidence that users understood or preferred it.

## Make the study materials preserve the intended condition

Before calling a protocol ready to run, trace each assigned task condition through
its prompt, fictional people/objects/data, stimulus versions, state transitions and
interpretation key. Keep those facts coherent across the materials; when another
condition changes a role or object, provide its matching material or an explicit
reset. Vary the hypothesis being tested while holding unrelated facts equivalent.
Otherwise a reasonable objection to contradictory data can look like a usability
failure. An answer key describes the simulated condition, not a desired participant
opinion or a guessed production rule.

Dry-run every materially different branch using the actual cards/screens and
facilitator instructions, including an interruption or ambiguous result when it
belongs to the question. Record which material appeared, what the facilitator did
and which observation would distinguish competing explanations. Revise a material
confound before interpreting it as a product finding. A desk check verifies only
the written scenario; label facilitator rehearsal and participant results separately
when they have not occurred. Keep the branch map in the existing research record,
not a second study specification.

## Convert evidence into a choice

For each consequential observation retain task/context → visible behavior →
impact → plausible cause(s) → revision → disconfirming check. Distinguish inability
to find an action, unclear wording, absent information and implementation failure;
each has a different owner. A participant disliking a color is preference evidence;
missing the primary action is behavioral evidence. Preserve both appropriately.

A study plan names the research question, participant fit, realistic goal/data,
neutral prompts, observation sheet, success/failure criteria and synthesis method.
Report actual sample and unobserved contexts. An expert session can inform a
prototype release with explicit limits; it cannot be labeled user validation.

Route the result to its owner: revise the Product Thesis for a disproved user or
outcome assumption, the Object/Content Model for terminology or relationship
failure, the journey/topology for findability and continuity failure, or the
Design Proposition for content, interaction or expression failure. Re-run the
smallest affected task and preserve unrelated decisions. A failed downstream
specimen may reopen an upstream decision; finishing a study phase never forces
forward progress.

Basis: [NN/g usability testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/)
distinguishes participant behavior from facilitator instructions. This decision
and evidence workflow is a synthesis for the existing Skill, not a claimed study.
